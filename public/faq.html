File Sorter FAQ

A human-in-the-loop assistant that reads inbound email attachments, suggests which case and folder they belong in, and waits for your approval before anything is saved to Dropbox.

Purpose & goals

The problem: Client documents arrive by email from all kinds of senders — medical records vendors, HR departments, e-sign services, relatives, listservs. Sorting them into the right case folder takes time and context.

What File Sorter does: It reads each email and attachment, uses AI to suggest a case and Dropbox subfolder, and posts a review card in Slack so a team member can confirm or correct before filing.

What it never does: It does not automatically save files. Every document requires an explicit Approve click. Low-confidence or ambiguous items are flagged for human review.

Human approval required. Think of the bot as a smart intake clerk — it prepares the filing recommendation; you make the final call.

How it works

1
Email arrives with attachment(s) Gmail / Workspace forwards mail with attachments to the File Sorter webhook.
2
AI analyzes the content Reads subject, body (including forwards), filename, and document text. Matches against known cases and staff hints.
3
One Slack card per email Multiple attachments from the same email appear on a single queue card in #file-sorter-queue.
4
You review in Slack Check the suggested case, folder, and confidence. Use buttons or thread commands to correct if needed.
5
Approve → saved to Dropbox All attachments in the email are filed. The case Slack channel gets a confirmation with the file attached.

What you see on a queue card

  • From / To / Subject / Received — email metadata
  • Attachment(s) — filename(s) and each suggested folder
  • AI Suggested Case — matched client case (Slack channel name)
  • AI Suggested Folder — Dropbox subfolder (Medical, Pleadings, etc.)
  • Confidence — how sure the AI is (below 75% → needs review)
  • Reason — short explanation of why it chose that case/folder

Cards marked Needs Human Review usually mean no case matched, low confidence, or the AI couldn't identify the PI client from the email alone.

Slack buttons

These appear on each queue card. Clicking a button is how you interact with the bot — there are no slash commands.

Primary action
Approve

Saves all attachments in this email to Dropbox under the suggested (or overridden) case and folder. Posts confirmation to the case Slack channel with the file attached.

Get help
Change Case/Folder

Posts instructions in the thread explaining how to override case, folder, or hints. Does not change anything by itself — reply in the thread, then click Approve.

Flag
Needs Attention

Marks the item for follow-up. You can still use Change Case/Folder and Approve when ready. Use when something looks off but you aren't ready to file or skip.

Skip
Do Not Sort

Closes the item without saving to Dropbox. Use for newsletters, spam, or mail that isn't a client document.

Thread commands

Before clicking Approve, reply in the card's thread with any of these. They are read when you Approve.

Override case or folder

case: 1277 case: Juan Garcia folder: Medical
  • case: — case number or client name
  • folder: — applies to all attachments in that email
Correspondence Expenses Intake Investigation LOP Lost Wages Medical PD Photos Pleadings Settlement Subrogation

Teach the system (saved for future emails)

Two types of hints — use the right one:

case hint: Maria Garcia emails on behalf of PI client Juan Garcia sort hint: Law360 newsletters from this listserv — Do Not Sort
  • case hint: — Who is the client? Relatives, nicknames, sender→client links. Helps match the right case.
  • sort hint: — How should mail from this sender be filed? Provider behavior, folder rules, newsletters to ignore.

Hints are saved when you Approve. The next email from that sender gets (usually) match better automatically.

Multiple attachments

One email with several attachments produces one Slack card, not one card per file. One Approve files everything to the same case.

  • Each file can have its own suggested folder unless you set a thread folder: override (then all files use that folder).
  • If the first attachment identifies the case, later attachments in the same email inherit that case.

Frequently asked questions

Why didn't the bot pick a case?

Common reasons: the PI client isn't named clearly in the email (sender is a vendor, not the client), the case isn't in the index yet, or the name only partially matches a different client. Use case hint: and case: in the thread, then Approve to teach it for next time.

What's the difference between Needs Attention and Needs Human Review?

Needs Human Review on the card title means the AI flagged uncertainty before anyone clicked anything — usually no case match or low confidence. Needs Attention (status) can also mean someone pressed the button, or a duplicate file was detected in Dropbox.

Can I file to a case the AI didn't suggest?

Yes. Reply in the thread with case: 1277 or case: Client Name, then Approve. The system saves a case hint for that sender so future mail may auto-match.

What emails are ignored?

Emails without attachments are skipped. Certain known list senders (e.g. TTLA listserv) are ignored at ingestion. Use sort hint: + Do Not Sort for other recurring non-client mail.

Where do files go after Approve?

Files upload to the case's Dropbox folder under the chosen subfolder (Medical, Pleadings, etc.). A summary message with the file is cross-posted to that case's Slack channel.

Does the bot read old Slack messages to update?

No. Only new inbound emails get the latest card layout and logic. Existing queue cards won't change after a deploy.
